## 1. The fundamental architecture

The building has a central structure. Two wings branch from it.

```
                    CENTRAL ENTRANCE
              Gate → Personalisation → Welcome door

                    CENTRAL ELEVATOR
     Main experience · identity · welcome · floor indicator
                        · navigation

        LEFT WING                          RIGHT WING
   project spaces and displays       project spaces and displays

                      CENTRAL EXIT
        Charter → Participation → Connection → Footer
```

This is conceptual structure, not a final architectural drawing — but it is not negotiable either. Everything else in this document is arranged around it.

Read vertically, it is a spine: you enter, you rise, you leave. Read horizontally, it is a building: the wings extend from the spine at every floor the lift stops at, and only there.

**The entrance and the exit are central.** They are not wings, not floors, and not destinations competing for space with projects. A visitor enters through the spine and leaves through the spine.

**Proportion is meaning.** The wings exist because the spine exists. They are not equal thirds of a webpage; they are extensions of a central structure, and the geometry in §6 is what makes that legible without a word of explanation.

## 2. Central structure ≠ wing content

This is the rule that prevents the last mistake from happening again, so it is written as a rule and not as a preference.

| Central functions | Wing spaces |
|---|---|
| Entrance | Community projects |
| Elevator | Creative showcases |
| Floor navigation | Project displays |
| Our Services | Events and initiatives |
| Membership | Contributors' work |
| Join the Network | Other project spaces |
| Announcements | |
| Exit and charter | |

Our Services, Membership and Join the Network are reached through the elevator's floating navigation. They open their own information panels or forms. They do not become wings, and they do not become additional floors.

The exit is also central. It is not a project occupying either wing.

**The test for anything new.** Before a new element is designed, it must answer one question: does it belong to the spine, or to a wing?

- If it is how the visitor moves, is welcomed, navigates, joins or leaves — it belongs to the spine.
- If it is something the visitor looks at, reads about or explores — it belongs to a wing.

No central function should be moved into a wing merely to fill space. No wing project should be mistaken for a central building function. Both mistakes make the building look like a page rather than a place.

### What must not happen

Seven failures this architecture exists to prevent, written as rules:

- Left-wing content must never accidentally appear in the right wing.
- A project must not be duplicated just because it has multiple names within one existing website.
- Adding a project must not create a new floor automatically.
- Adding a floor must not require rewriting the elevator.
- The marquee must not become mixed into project listings.
- Project frames must not crowd the elevator.
- Removing or replacing one project must not damage the other project spaces.

## 3. The central entrance

The entrance is its own complete opening experience, and it happens inside the spine alone. While the visitor is at the gate, there are no wings beside them — only the central structure and soft light on either side.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | The Pure Love Network State seal glows at the centre of the closed gate — the gate is the only background behind it |
| 2 | The seal fades away from the centre |
| 3 | The gate opens, revealing the personalised entry capsule |
| 4 | A central capsule asks for two things only: the visitor's name, and their chosen colour. Neither is saved to a database; both remain available for as long as the visit lasts |
| 5 | The capsule disappears and the welcome door becomes the main visual surface |
| 6 | **First typing:** the visitor's name in their chosen colour, followed by the approved personal welcome |
| 7 | Directly below the welcome, the Pure Love Community Network of States logo fades in inside a pill-shaped presentation, then holds a gentle breathing glow |
| 8 | **Second typing:** *Pure Love Community Network of States Virtual Experience* |
| 9 | COMMENCE / ENTER appears — and only now, after the second typing has finished |

**Two typing sequences with the logo between them.** The first belongs to the visitor: their name, in their colour, followed by a welcome addressed to them. The second belongs to the network: its own identity. The logo sits between the two because they are two different statements, and running them together would make the visitor's welcome look like the opening line of the network's title. The rhythm is deliberate — a greeting, then a name.

**The button waits.** COMMENCE / ENTER does not appear while either sequence is still typing. A control that appears early invites the visitor to skip the one moment the entrance exists to give them.

**The entry capsule.** The specification describes the capsule as purple. That is a single accent on the entry form, not a change to the building's palette — the visitor's colour still never repaints the building (decisions 9 and 10). Its exact tone is confirmed against the artwork in Stage B like every other starting colour, and the capsule itself disappears once it has done its work.

There are no left and right wings at this stage. No project displays beside the gate. No side entrance. Nothing at the edges competing with the arrival.

The reception sits inside this central entrance — it is where the visitor is received, not a level the lift travels to.

**How the entrance was built before, and why it is on hold.** The first entrance prototype is written and running. Its layout is central, but it was specified while the blueprint still described the building in horizontal terms, so it is not trusted as the foundation. It is paused rather than extended (see §17), and it will be rebuilt against the geometry in §6 once that geometry is confirmed.

## 4. The central elevator

The elevator is the main environment from which visitors experience and navigate the building. It is not a control panel bolted to the side of a page; it is the room the visitor stands in.

It contains:

- personalised welcome;
- Pure Love Network State identity;
- transparent architectural surroundings;
- current-floor and approaching-floor indicator;
- direction of travel;
- floor selection;
- circular navigation control;
- floating navigation menu;
- separate announcement marquee;
- subtle decorative features.

### What is inside it — an elegant office reception

The lift must feel like a quiet reception room with a view, not like a conventional lift and not like a futuristic control room. Nothing inside it should read as machinery.

| Element | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Floor indicator** | Centrally positioned, above the framed welcome display, with the clock part of the same arrangement. Visually prominent, but never competing with the welcome |
| **Framed welcome display** | Continuously types and gently loops a short welcome. This is a **second, different** message from the entrance welcome — brief, and it repeats |
| **Visitor's name** | Set in the colour chosen at the entrance, and only in that colour |
| **Clock** | Above the welcome display, integrated into the indicator arrangement rather than floating on its own |
| **Flower stand** | A subtle silhouette, in the visitor's chosen colour |
| **Lift panel** | Floating floor-selection controls |
| **Announcement marker** | A floating navigation circle or door, opening announcements — an entrance to a place, not a list item |
| **Glass interior** | Clear, so the wings stay legible through it and the lift never feels enclosed |

**The panel must say which way the building goes.** Every destination is above the ground floor. The panel therefore reads as a vertical sequence with the ground floor at the bottom and the final destination at the top, so the visitor never has to work out whether the building continues below them.

**Movement is felt as distance, not as a page swap.** The indicator counts the floors as the lift travels, the current floor stays named, and the approach preview (§7) appears before the last stop. That is what makes the lift a journey rather than a menu with a fade.

**Upward is the journey, not a cage.** Scrolling through the building carries the lift upward, and the panel is how any floor is reached. Downward travel is always available by choosing a floor, and the indicator names the floor, the direction and the destination, so a return visit never feels like a mistake (decision 53).

The elevator remains central. The wings are architectural spaces extending from it.

**The glass matters.** Because the capsule is transparent, the wings are visible from inside it before the visitor steps out to them. The lift is how the building is understood: you see where you are, what is beside you, and what is coming.

### The circular control — navigation that grows out of the logo

The logo the visitor meets as the glowing seal at the gate returns inside the lift as a small circle on the **left** side of the lift, at the start of the marquee band. There it is not decoration, and it is not a menu button standing beside a menu: **it is the origin of the navigation.**

- At rest, the circle is the only thing visible.
- Activating it releases the destination list, which expands out of the circle. The list is not a bar that happened to sit next to the logo, and there is no second, permanent menu for it to duplicate.
- The expansion is an unfolding, not a fade: the circle gives the list its origin, and the list returns to it when closed.
- The list is read as a column of illuminated destinations — the floors in order, the final floor marked as final, and the actions kept apart from them.
- Choosing a destination, or touching the circle again, closes it. Nothing stays open while the visitor is looking at a wing.
- The lift stays the visual centre of the building; the circle is its discreet access point, not a second centre of gravity.

The column is ordered **upward**, as the lift panel already is (decision 45): the ground floor sits at the bottom and the final floor at the top, so the control and the room never disagree about which way the building goes. Every floor below is derived from the floor records, never stored, and a project with no recorded floor simply does not appear. The layout is illustrative rather than final artwork:

```
               PURE LOVE NETWORK
                Elevator control          ○

             ── Select destination ──

   ●   Final floor    Gift of Remembrance · 97 Mega
   ○   Second floor   Podcast and Co. · WhatsApp Channel
   ○   First floor    97 Arts Galaxy · World Within
   ○   Ground floor   97 Community Hub · 97 Industry

             ── ── ── ── ── ── ──

   S   Services       short information popup
   M   Membership     membership panel
   J   Register / Join collaboration application
   A   Announcements  announcement panel
   E   Exit           leave the building
```

**The lights are the same truth as the indicator.** Each destination carries a light; the current floor's light is lit while the others stay visible, so the visitor can always see where they could go. As the lift travels, the lit light moves — and the indicator inside the lift and the control must always read the same floor (decision 51).

**Actions are not levels.** Services and Exit sit below the floor list, set apart and unnumbered. Services opens its short popup, Exit leaves the building. Membership and Join the Network remain actions reached from the same control. None of them is a destination in the floor records, none appears in the indicator, and none is ever counted as a storey (decision 52).

**It sits on the left, and the marquee leaves it from there.** The circle belongs to the left of the lift; the announcements emerge from it and travel left to right; and the band is never wider than the lift it belongs to, on desktop and on mobile alike (decision 56). This is deliberately not the arrangement of the existing World Within website, where the mark sits on the right: the building takes a reference without borrowing the interface. Nothing replaces the circle on the right, either — World Within is a project destination in a wing, not an ornament beside the elevator. The exact offset of the circle inside the lift's width belongs to the stage drawing, and it is no longer an open decision.

### Floors and actions are different kinds of navigation

The floor indicator answers *where am I*. The controls answer *what can I do*. They are never merged into one list, and no action is ever given a floor of its own.

The indicator always communicates: the floor the visitor is on, the floor they are approaching, the direction of travel, and that another floor can be selected.

Selecting a floor moves the elevator. Everything else opens a popup over the building and leaves the visitor standing exactly where they are.

| Control | Function |
|---|---|
| **Floor navigation** | Destination / floor selector — moves the elevator |
| **Our Services** | Short information popup explaining services, what occupying a space means, and the ways to participate |
| **Membership** | The membership panel and its form (terms still open) |
| **Register / Join the Network** | The free-will collaboration application (decision 54) |
| **Announcements** | New projects, updates, events — the same records the marquee reads |
| **Exit** | The building's departure destination — a place to reach, not a level (§5, §13) |

There is **one** destination list, not two: it is the lift's floating panel, and the circular logo is how it is released. Both wings always update together to match the selected floor, and the list, the indicator and the lit destination light never disagree (decision 51).

None of the five below the floors is a storey. They are unnumbered, they are never counted, they never appear in the indicator, and choosing one never changes the lift's current floor (decision 52).

The elevator's proportions and animation are independent of how many floors exist. The floor list is read from data, so adding a floor adds a destination and a position without changing the lift's design. Destinations are calculated from the floor records rather than hardcoded as a fixed number of stops.

## 5. The central exit

After the final destination, the visitor rises into the exit — still inside the spine. The wings end below; the exit is the spine's last room.

The exit is also a destination of the lift control, listed below the floors and marked *leave the building*, and it is never numbered as a level (decision 52). The visitor can see it waiting from the final floor and can reach it when they choose.

The heart of the exit is an interaction, not a footer at the bottom of a long page: the visitor is invited to look at what the network stands for before they leave. The side information panel, the charter and the footer all belong here, in the centre. Details in §13.

The exit is visible from the final floor — it is the last room in the spine, not a hidden page — but the visitor is invited to explore both destinations first. Leaving should feel like the completion of a journey, not an interruption of one. Exploration is invited, never forced.

## 6. Proportions — the working geometry

This is the part that had to be fixed before any layout code was written. The numbers below are the settled geometry: exact, testable, measured at four screen widths, and recorded so the code cannot quietly decide them.

### Horizontal composition — desktop

| Zone | Share of stage width | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Left wing** | 32% | Project spaces and displays |
| **Central spine** | 36% | Entrance · elevator · exit |
| **Right wing** | 32% | Project spaces and displays |
| **Marquee track** | 48px band at the base of the spine, never wider than the lift | Announcements, separate from everything |

The spine is slightly wider than a wing on purpose. The lift has to carry the identity, the welcome, the name, the indicator, the circular control and the navigation panel, and almost all of that is type that has to fit inside a capsule. The surplus is 27–51px depending on the screen, and it is spent entirely in the one zone that holds text.

The two wings are identical in width. A visitor should never feel that one side of the building is the real building and the other is an afterthought — even where one wing carries a larger project.

**Separation.** No pillars. A 1px champagne-silver seam where each wing meets the spine, plus a soft shadow falling *into* the wing, so the wings read as recessed behind the glass rather than pasted beside it. The spine is the nearest thing to the visitor; the wings sit behind it.

### 36 / 32 / 32 against equal thirds — the comparison, settled

Both options were measured at the same four stage widths, with the capsule at 78% of the spine and the wing grid at three columns with the documented padding and gutter.

| Stage width | Spine 36% | Capsule | Frame | Spine 33.3% | Capsule | Frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1024px | 369px | 288px | 90 × 119 | 341px | 266px | 93 × 124 |
| 1280px | 461px | 359px | 112 × 149 | 427px | 333px | 117 × 156 |
| 1440px | 518px | 404px | 126 × 168 | 480px | 374px | 131 × 175 |
| 1920px | 691px | 539px | 168 × 224 | 640px | 499px | 175 × 233 |

Read as a trade: the 36% spine takes 27–51px more width and gives the capsule 21–40px more, at a cost of 4–7px on each frame edge — a constant **7.8% of frame area** at every width, because both options shrink in proportion and neither changes the slot count.

**Why 7.8% of frame area is the cheaper side of the trade.** The wings keep six frames either way. A frame loses about 5px of width on a laptop, inside an image that is already the largest thing in its own slot; nobody reads that as a smaller picture. The spine gains 27px of width where a floor name, a welcome line and a control row are set in type — where 27px is the difference between a line that fits and a line that wraps.

**Why not equal thirds.** At 33.3 / 33.3 / 33.3 the building is three columns of identical standing. That is the composition v3.0 was written to reject: it makes the spine one partner among two wings, when the wings are supposed to be extensions of it. The proportions would contradict §1 in the one place a visitor reads structure without being told anything. 36/32/32 is the smallest honest asymmetry — the spine is 12.5% wider than a wing, so the centre visibly carries more weight than either side, while **the wings stay identical to each other**, which is the symmetry that actually matters (decision 8).

**The honest limit.** Four percentage points on their own are a quiet difference, and they are not carrying the hierarchy alone: the seams, the shadow falling into the wings, the inset capsule, and the entrance and exit — where the wings disappear and the spine keeps its full width — do most of the work. If a stronger centre is ever wanted, the spine rises to 38–40% and both wings fall together. **One wing is never widened to correct the balance.**

**The narrow-width limit is not this choice.** At 1024px the three-column gallery gives 90px frames under either option. Below roughly 1200px it is the wing grid, not the spine split, that needs changing — which is what the tablet and mobile rules in §6 and §16 already do.

**Settled:** spine 36%, wings 32% each, capsule 78% of the spine by 74% of the stage, six slots at 3:4, 48px marquee band. Locked as decision 39.

### The data hierarchy — declared

The building is five levels deep, and each level owns only its own thing. This is the foundational decision of the whole build, and it comes before any floor record carries a project.

| Level | Owns | Does not own |
|---|---|---|
| **Building** | The order of the floors, and nothing else | Any project content |
| **Floor** | A unique floor id, a name, a left wing, a right wing, its own display and navigation configuration | The entries inside a wing |
| **Wing** | A unique wing id, its position, its own collection of entries, optional duplication-origin information | The project's copy, artwork or destination |
| **Wing entry** | A unique entry id, a project reference, its own display configuration, its own description and destination overrides | Where else that project appears |
| **Project content** | Artwork, name, short and long descriptions, action label and link | Which floor or wing is showing it |

**The rule this replaces.** A floor record that holds a bare list of project ids — `projects: ['…']` — can only say *this project appears here*. It cannot say *this copy of the project has its own wording, its own slot or its own order*, and it has nowhere to record that a wing was copied from another wing. That is why the **entry** sits between the wing and the project: the wing is a space, the project is a library object, and the entry is the thing that belongs to the space.

**A floor record** holds a unique id, a label, two wings and its own display and navigation configuration. It stores no project content and no floor number.

**A wing record** holds a unique id, its position (`left` or `right`), a heading, a configuration (`gallery` or `landmark`), an optional `originWingId`, and its own array of entries.

**A wing entry** holds a unique id, a `projectRef` into the shared project library, its slot and span, an optional `originEntryId`, and its own overrides — used only where this copy differs from the library entry.

**Nothing in this hierarchy needs an image.** Entries can be declared, ordered and duplicated before a single artwork exists; image references attach to the project library later, in Stage E.

### Duplicating a wing — decided

Duplicating a wing creates a new, independent wing. It is seeded from the source and then free to diverge.

The duplicate keeps:

- its own wing id;
- its own entry ids;
- the id of the wing it was copied from (`originWingId`);
- a reference to each origin entry (`originEntryId`), so provenance is never lost;
- its own ordering and membership;
- its own content snapshot or overrides.

What that buys: removing, adding, rearranging or editing an entry in the duplicate never silently modifies the original, and editing the original never silently changes the duplicate. The project library stays the shared source of reusable projects; the duplicate's entries are its own records, with the original retained as provenance.

**Why by copy and not by reference.** A wing is a *space*, and spaces are renovatable independently (decisions 11 and 12). Duplication by reference would make two rooms that can never differ — which is a second name for one room.

This is implemented in `js/floors.js` before any floor record carries content (§15).

### One template, unlimited floors

Every project floor is the same arrangement: left wing, lift, right wing. The floor renderer is written once and creates that arrangement for every floor record, so a new floor is a record and not a page.

- Add a floor record with a label and two wings.
- The renderer draws the same architecture around it.
- The elevator reads the updated list, so destinations, indicator numbers and travel distances follow automatically.
- A new project never creates a floor; a new floor never requires rewriting the elevator.

Floor positions are calculated from the floor records rather than stored as fixed stops. Inserting a floor between two others renumbers what comes above it and changes nothing else.

### What arrives with a floor change

A floor change is a movement, not a page load. In order: the lift travels (about 900ms) while the indicator counts the floors; the arriving floor's heading plaques fade in; the frames appear in sequence, 60ms apart; the previous floor's frames are gone before the new ones settle. Nothing fades through nothing — the wings are always either clearly leaving or clearly arriving.

### The rule this protects

A health initiative by one contributor and a health initiative by another can appear *alongside each other* on the same floor. Nobody's work displaces anybody else's, and your own work never gets pushed to the bottom of a growing list. That is the whole point of fixed floors — and of slots that belong to the building rather than to whoever filled them last.

## 8. Decisions that are locked

These are settled. Build on them; do not reopen them without a deliberate decision.

1. **The building is a central spine with two wings branching from it.** Entrance, elevator and exit are central. Projects live in the wings.
2. **Central structure ≠ wing content.** No central function is given a wing to fill space; no wing project is treated as a building function.
3. **The entrance sequence** — glowing seal, seal fades, gate opens, personalisation capsule, welcome door, the two typed sequences, COMMENCE / ENTER.
4. **The entrance happens in the spine alone** — no wings in view, no side entrance, no project displays beside the gate.
5. **Reveal timing** — text and messages appear only when the visitor actually reaches that point. Nothing is pre-displayed.
6. **Typewriter welcome** — the personalised message types itself into existence.
7. **Transparent elevator** as the centre of the experience and the visitor's viewpoint, showing the wings through glass.
8. **Symmetry of the wings** — identical width, identical structure, neither subordinate.
9. **The visitor's colour is theirs** — used for their name and their personal elements only, never repainting the building.
10. **The building has its own permanent palette**, independent of any visitor.
11. **Fixed floors, updateable spaces** — no new floor per contributor, and no new floor per project.
12. **Fixed slots inside fixed floors** — a space can be filled, featured, replaced, renovated or moved; the grid does not move with it.
13. **Reusable project frame** — artwork, integrated bottom gradient, name, short description, one action.
14. **Two display states** — the frame in the building, and the expanded popup.
15. **Artwork and data are independent** — an image can be redesigned without touching the destination link or the architecture.
16. **The elevator shows the current floor** at all times — functional requirement, not decoration.
17. **The elevator owns the navigation**: separate floor selector, Services, Membership, Join the Network, Announcements. These are not one combined popup.
18. **Services is a short information popup, not a floor.**
19. **Membership is a short form, not a long registration.**
20. **The marquee supports both linked and unlinked messages**, and lives in its own band at the base of the lift — at the edge of the lift, never wider than it, and never inside a wing.
21. **The exit is central** — an interaction, then a side panel, then the footer. Not a wing, not a page bottom.
22. **The visitor is never forced** to join, contribute, pay or sign up in order to explore.
23. **External JavaScript files**, modular CSS, data-driven content. No large inline script block.
24. **The visitor's colour is session state**, not an account.
25. **The floor map** — ground: 97 Community Hub / 97 Industry; first intermediate floor: 97 Arts Galaxy / World Within; second intermediate floor: Podcast and Co. / WhatsApp Channel; further intermediate floors for new projects, each assignment recorded explicitly before use; final destination: Gift of Remembrance / 97 Mega facing each other; then the exit. Reception belongs to the entrance.
26. **Floors and elevator destinations are different kinds of navigation** — selecting a floor moves the elevator; Services, Membership, Register / Join the Network, Announcements and Exit open their own popup or destination and leave the lift standing where it is.
27. **An existing website is a project display, not a floor.**
28. **97 Creative Management is one organisation, one website, one display.** 97 Arts Galaxy is the name presented inside its own website, and the building uses that name as the heading of the wing that shows the website — never as a second gallery, a second website or a second display.
29. **Existing projects are presented as they are:** 97 Creative Management (one display, shown under the 97 Arts Galaxy heading), World Within (one display, its own wing), Podcast and Co., the WhatsApp Channel, Gift of Remembrance (landmark, final floor, one wing), 97 Mega (landmark, opposite wing, deliberately prominent).
30. **Attribution is part of the work.** The 97 Creative Management watermark on the artwork of African people's work is preserved exactly — never cropped, covered by the gradient, or removed.
31. **Warm ivory is the dominant tone.** Champagne silver carries the structure, brushed gold appears only in fine details, clear glass carries the lift. Soft ash stays as a supporting neutral.
32. **Decoration is restrained.** Soft architectural lighting, reflections on the glass, occasional petals. No continuous ambient motion, nothing that glows behind text, nothing that competes with project artwork.
33. **The geometry is decided before the code.** Widths, heights and slot counts live in one place — custom properties at the top of `architecture.css` — not scattered through stylesheets.
34. **One floor template, unlimited floor records.** A new floor is a record with two wings; the renderer is written once.
35. **Data and rendering are separate.** Floor assignments live in `floors.js`, project content in `projects.js`, announcements in `announcements.js`. The renderer holds no content.
36. **The elevator does not know how many floors there are.** Destinations, indicator numbers and travel distances are calculated from the floor records.
37. **Left and right are working positions**, held in data and confirmable, swappable without architectural change.
38. **Nothing is coded before its stage.** The proportions are settled (39), the data hierarchy is declared (40) and duplication is decided (41); content still waits on the floor list's remaining assignments.
39. **The geometry is settled at 36% / 32% / 32%** — measured against equal thirds and chosen for what it says about the centre, not for extra room. The wings remain identical to each other; if the centre ever needs more weight, the spine rises and both wings fall together, never one wing alone.
40. **The data hierarchy is fixed at five levels: building → floors → wings → wing entries → project content.** A floor holds two wings; a wing holds its own entries; an entry points at a project in the shared library. A floor never depends on a bare list of project ids (§7).
41. **Duplication is by copy, not by reference.** A duplicated wing is a new independent wing: its own wing id, its own entry ids, its origin wing and origin entries recorded, its own order, membership and overrides. Editing the copy never changes the original, and editing the original never changes the copy. The project library stays shared provenance.
42. **Mobile is its own arrangement, not a compressed desktop.** The lift is the full content width, each wing is a full-width view that slides away and returns, and the floor indicator is never hidden. There is no simultaneous three-column compression on a phone.
43. **The entrance has two typing sequences, separated by the community logo.** First the visitor's name in their chosen colour followed by the approved personal welcome; then, below the logo, the network's identity. The logo sits between them because the first statement is about the visitor and the second about the network. COMMENCE / ENTER appears only after the second sequence has finished.
44. **The welcome door and the elevator door are two separate architectural elements.** The visitor enters through the welcome door, the welcome presentation clears, and only then are the closed lift doors discovered and opened onto the glass interior and the ground floor. One is never used to stand in for the other.
45. **The lift interior is an elegant office reception**, not a conventional lift and not a futuristic control room: a framed looping welcome, the visitor's name in their colour, a clock above the welcome within the floor-indicator arrangement, a flower-stand silhouette in their colour, a floating floor panel that reads upward from the ground floor, an announcement door, and clear glass. The indicator is prominent but never competes with the welcome.
46. **The lift's welcome is a second, looping message** — short, quiet and different from the entrance welcome, repeating for as long as the visitor is inside.
47. **Mobile moves in two directions and never confuses them.** A wing is reached horizontally — the indicator beside the lift, a swipe, or a slide — and a floor is reached vertically upward in the lift. The wing indicators update with the floor, and a persistent control returns the visitor to the lift.
48. **The final floor's two destinations are declared, not inferred.** Gift of Remembrance and 97 Mega are named as the two wings of the uppermost floor in the data, with their sides explicit; nothing about the final floor is derived from the order of a list. The sides are recorded: Gift of Remembrance in the left wing, 97 Mega in the right (decision 59).
49. **The circular logo is the origin of lift navigation, not a button beside a menu.** The mark that appears as the seal at the gate returns inside the lift as a circle at the end of the marquee band. Activating the circle releases the destination list, which expands out of it; choosing a destination, or touching the circle again, closes it. There is no separate permanent menu for it to duplicate, and no navigation bar that merely happens to sit beside the logo. The lift remains the visual centre of the building and the circle its discreet access point.
50. **Three elements, three voices.** The marquee announces, the circular control navigates, the floor indicator locates. The marquee never becomes a navigation menu, the control never carries announcements, and the indicator never lists projects. Each keeps its own band, its own type and its own job, so none competes with the artwork or with the others.
51. **The destination lights are one truth, shown twice.** Every destination in the control carries a light. The current floor's light is lit and the others stay visible rather than going dark, so the visitor can always see where they could go. As the lift travels the lit light moves, and the in-lift indicator and the control must always read the same floor; if the two ever disagree, the control is wrong, because the lift knows where it is.
52. **The control's destinations are not floors.** The lift has physical floors and separate destinations. **Services, Membership, Register / Join the Network, Announcements and Exit** are not storeys: they stay unnumbered, are never counted as floors, never change the lift's current floor, and open their own popup or destination experience without the lift moving. Register / Join leads to the collaboration application (decision 54); Exit is the building's departure destination, not another level (§5, §13).
53. **The journey is upward, the panel reads upward, and the top is permanent.** The building is travelled from the ground floor upward: scrolling carries the lift up, the floor panel reads from ground at the bottom to the final floor at the top, and the indicator names the current floor, the direction and the destination. Downward travel is always permitted by choosing a floor — the upward arrangement expresses the intended journey, not a restriction. The final floor, Gift of Remembrance and 97 Mega, one per wing, is the uppermost storey immediately before the exit: not a temporary assignment, not an optional future floor, and never inferred from the order of a list. Its sides are recorded as well (decision 59), so the pair, its position and its arrangement across the lift are all fixed.
54. **Register / Join the Network is a free-will collaboration, not a purchase.** It invites people who want to become contributors, collaborators or owners of a project space: a left-wing or right-wing space, an existing website or body of work, a service or project announcement, resources, knowledge, time, expertise, ideas, opportunities, or a contribution nobody has listed yet. Joining is an agreement to pursue mutual growth in which both sides can name the value they receive; it is never built on exploitation, compulsory payment, or an unequal arrangement dressed as partnership. The form is deliberately short — name, email, how you would like to participate, what you would like to contribute, and what would make the collaboration mutually beneficial — the participation field may suggest but must also accept free text, the contribution field stays open-ended, and a plain acknowledgement confirms that participation is voluntary and rests on dignity, mutual benefit, peace and freedom from prejudice. Nothing in it may suggest that a contribution buys ownership or guarantees a permanent wing; a space is established only after both parties agree.
55. **The project frame is permanently composed, and its action sits at the outer edge.** Every project, in either wing, uses one composition: the image dominant, its subject toward the extreme ends where appropriate, breathing space in the centre, a dark gradient developing toward the bottom deep enough for readable text without hiding the artwork; title and a short introduction sit over that gradient, with a subtle indication that more is available. One action sits at the **extreme outer edge** of the frame — far left for the left wing, far right for the right wing — never close to the lift. Choosing it opens a popup over the current floor with the image, the description and the destination link; closing it returns the visitor to the same floor and the lift never moves. Text colour defaults to high contrast, with an optional project accent and automatic or explicitly configured contrast against the gradient; it is part of a project's display settings, never a mandatory question at upload. The image is uploaded only after the frame structure exists.
56. **The circular control belongs to the left of the lift, and the marquee begins there.** The control sits on the left side; the announcements emerge from it and flow left to right; the band never exceeds the lift's width, on desktop or mobile; and the circle and strip never obstruct the lift or its central space. This is deliberately unlike the existing World Within site, where the mark sits on the right — a reference for identity, not an interface to copy. No project takes the other side of the lift: World Within is a wing destination, not an ornament beside the elevator.
57. **The building's project identities are fixed.** 97 Community Hub · 97 Industry · 97 Arts Galaxy · World Within · Podcast and Co. · the WhatsApp Channel · Gift of Remembrance · 97 Mega, with 97 Creative Management as the organisation whose website 97 Arts Galaxy is presented inside. No name is invented, substituted or merged, and a project's floor and side are recorded explicitly before they are shown — never guessed from a list, a conversation or a count. A project with no recorded floor does not appear in the lift control.
58. **Every project entry is independently manageable; the library stays shared.** The left and right wings use the same presentation system, and each wing owns its own collection of entries, its own ordering and its own display configuration. Duplicating a wing copies it into an independent collection that keeps its references and provenance, so changing one wing never silently alters the other. A new project, or a new collaborator's project, is added without rewriting the building.
59. **The last floor's sides are confirmed.** **Gift of Remembrance stands in the left wing and 97 Mega in the right**, facing each other across the lift. The pair itself was already permanent (decision 53); this records which wing holds which. The assignment lives only in the floor data, so turning the two around is one edit and never a rebuild — but nothing appears on this floor that is not one of these two projects.
60. **Podcast and Co. and the WhatsApp Channel share the second floor.** Both identities are recorded on one further intermediate floor, above 97 Arts Galaxy / World Within and below the final destination: **Podcast and Co. on the left, the WhatsApp Channel on the right**, one wing each, both gallery spaces. Their floor and sides are recorded rather than inferred, so both now appear in the lift control, and each keeps its own heading, entries and destination rather than being merged into one space. Both links are recorded now (§7, decision 61); their artwork and copy are still to come (§18). A further project joins as a new floor or a new wing, always by an explicit record — never by being mentioned (decision 57).

61. **Every destination in the building is recorded with its live link.** The eight spaces are not placeholders: each one's address is recorded beside its floor and side, in one place, so nothing downstream has to guess, shorten or reconstruct a URL — 97 Community Hub, 97 Industry, 97 Arts Galaxy, World Within, Podcast and Co., the WhatsApp Channel, Gift of Remembrance and 97 Mega (§7). A link is content, not structure, so re-pointing one changes one line and never a layout. The network's own two addresses — the Pure Love Network State site and the Substack community — are recorded too, and they belong to the footer and the exit only, never to a wing, a floor or a project frame.

62. **The 150 / 2,000 figures do not exist as project requirements.** They appeared in an earlier conversational transcription and were never established as amounts, prices, fees or membership terms. They must not be used in the building, the membership structure, pricing or documentation, and they are not a question waiting for an answer: no decision is pending on them. No price is published anywhere in the building, and none is invented to fill the gap.

63. **The building is one application in the project's GitHub repository, served by GitHub Pages.** It is built from HTML, CSS, JavaScript and external assets, assembled and maintained in that repository, and published through GitHub Pages. Nothing about it is built around a system that keeps all images, descriptions, scripts or page structures inside one large document, and no part of it depends on a page builder or a hosted editor that would have to be reverse-engineered to change a picture. The implementation stays lightweight, organised and editable through the repository itself (decisions 23, 35, 69).

64. **Every visual asset is a separate file, referenced by its repository-relative path.** Project images, logos, decorative graphics and other artwork are uploaded into organised folders in the repository, and each JavaScript record stores only the path — `assets/projects/community-hub.png`, for example — never the picture. Replacing an image is replacing or re-pathing a file; the layout, the floor and the lift are untouched. Image files are never embedded as Base64 data, and image contents never sit inside a project record (decision 15).

65. **The eight existing records keep their identity and their destination, and are completed rather than recreated.** The records and links already in the shared library remain the same identities. Adding an image path, a short description, a full description, a logo or a further link to a record never creates a second record for the same project, and never re-points a link that is already correct. Each project's website and identity marks stay attached to the record they belong to (decisions 57, 61).

66. **Explore is an active control only once its popup exists, and the popup carries what the frame cannot.** The initial display stays restrained: image, title, short introduction where appropriate, and the action. The popup opens over the current floor carrying the larger image shown whole, the full description with enough detail to explain what the project actually offers, the project logo or identity mark, the link to the project's own website, and any other destination recorded for it. The full description is never shortened to fit the frame, and the frame is never treated as a shortened popup — the two states serve different purposes (decision 14). An action whose popup is not implemented yet is drawn inert: never a button that looks functional and does nothing, and never an action deleted from the frame to avoid the question.

67. **One reusable display and one reusable popup render every project, and the wings are explicit.** Project content is data-driven: a record identifies its project, its image path, its short and full descriptions, its actions and its identity mark, and each wing entry places that project in a named wing with an explicit side. The same component draws every frame and the same popup explains every project, in either wing, on any floor. Editing a project's image path or description edits one record and never rebuilds the floor, the wing or the elevator, and left and right are written down rather than inferred from an order (decisions 35, 37, 58).

68. **The elevator is the building's next major component, and it is architecture rather than a container.** It is what makes the two wings feel like one building, so it is never treated as an ordinary page container or as a decorative picture. Its structure, its floor indicator, its destination selection and its floating functions — Services, Membership, Join the Network and Announcements — are developed as one element working with the floor records, and those functions stay distinct from actual floor destinations (decisions 17, 26, 52). A still glass lift shape may stand in as a first visual stage, but only as a stage: what it stands in for is real floor navigation, travelled upward, with the indicator and the control never disagreeing about where the visitor is (decisions 49–51, 53).

69. **The building stays light by separation, not by promise.** HTML holds the structure, CSS holds the architectural appearance and the responsive layout, JavaScript holds the floor data, the project records, the elevator behaviour, the navigation and the interactions, images are separate files, and links are references on the right project record. Adding a floor, a project, an image or an announcement is an edit to the relevant record and its asset; it is never a rewrite of the application and never duplicated page code (decisions 23, 34, 35).

70. **Every page is labelled with the file that owns each change.** Each HTML page carries written edit points at the places that matter: an orientation block at the top naming the file behind each kind of change, and a label beside each place a record is loaded or a floor is mounted, saying which file to edit and what it controls. A page never leaves a script tag or a mount point unexplained, so someone opening the repository can find the right file without tracing the code. The labels also state where content does **not** live: no project name, description, link or image path is written into the HTML, because those belong to the records and would be lost the next time a floor is redrawn (decisions 63, 64, 69).

71. **The colour chooser offers every colour, not a list of colours.** The entrance's colour control is never a closed palette. The named colours remain as starting points, and the last control opens the browser's own colour field, which carries the whole visible spectrum — so a visitor is never told that their colour is not available, and the colour they wear is genuinely theirs (decisions 9, 24, 43). Whatever is chosen there is used exactly as a preset would be: the visitor's name, their welcome mark and their personal accents, and nothing else in the building. Choosing nothing still leaves the visitor with the building's gold, and the chooser never becomes a required step.

## 9. The visitor journey

Six scenes. Each one only exists when the visitor reaches it.

### Scene 1 — The gate
The Pure Love Network State logo appears as a glowing seal, inside the central spine. It fades. The gate opens. This is a moment of arrival, not a loading screen — keep it short and quiet.

### Scene 2 — Personalisation
A small popup emerges *from the building* and asks two things only: their name, and their chosen colour. Nothing else. No email, no account, no reason required. The colour is not a list to pick from: the named swatches are starting points, and the last control opens the full spectrum, so any colour at all can be worn (decision 71).

### Scene 3 — The welcome door
The entrance door appears centrally, the capsule is gone, and the door becomes the surface everything is written on. Then, in two separate typings: their name in their chosen colour, followed by the personal welcome; the community logo fading in below it inside its pill, holding a gentle breathing glow; then the network's identity. COMMENCE / ENTER appears only after the second typing has finished.

### Scene 4 — Transition into the lift
COMMENCE / ENTER is chosen. The welcome presentation fades and the stage clears for a moment — long enough for the visitor to notice the change of surface. The closed lift doors are now visible, and they slide open onto the glass interior, revealing the ground floor and its two wings. No walking animation, no character: a transition, not a simulation. The welcome door and the lift door are two thresholds, and the visitor crosses both.

### Scene 5 — The elevator and the floors
The visitor stands inside the transparent capsule. Around them: the floor indicator above a looping framed welcome, the clock, their name in their colour, the flower stand, the circular navigation control, the floating navigation panel, the announcement door and the marquee at the base. Through the glass: two wings. The visitor can travel up *and* down, revisiting spaces at will. The main journey leads upward; before the last stop, the panel announces the final destination and both of its rooms, so arriving is the answer to something the visitor already saw.

### Scene 6 — The exit
After the last destination, the lift rises into the exit. It opens a side information panel: what the network stands for, the charter, principles, collaboration guidelines, consent and participation, an invitation to join, and an optional way to support. The footer follows the exit, in the spine.

## 10. The project display system

### The frame — anatomy

```
┌─────────────────────────┐
│                         │
│      PROJECT ARTWORK    │   ← dominant, uncropped as far as the
│                         │     3:4 frame allows
│  ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░   │   ← gradient begins before the text
│  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓   │
│  ████ Project Name ███  │
│  ██ short intro line █  │
│  ██         [ Explore ] │   ← the action sits at the outer edge,
└─────────────────────────┘      away from the lift
```

**On the frame itself, only three things:** project name, brief description, one action (*Explore* / *Learn More*). Nothing else competes with the artwork.

The frame belongs to the wing, not to the project. It is the same frame at every slot on every floor, which is what allows a project to be moved or replaced without the wall changing shape.

### The action sits at the outer edge

The two wings extend away from the centre, and their interaction points belong at their outer ends. On the left wing the action sits at the frame's extreme left; on the right wing, at its extreme right. Neither button faces the lift, and neither drifts toward the centre when a wing is narrower or when a phone slides it into view.

That single rule does most of the building's visual balancing. It keeps the lift unobstructed without needing a barrier, keeps the two wings from reaching toward each other, and makes it obvious which frame an action belongs to even when six frames stand side by side. It is also what makes the building's arrangement its own, rather than a copy of an interface where controls cluster near the middle.

**Text colour is a display setting, not a question.** Every project receives a high-contrast default for text over the gradient; a project may optionally carry its own accent colour; contrast is either calculated against the gradient or set explicitly. Choosing a colour is never a mandatory step when a project is added — the system supplies a readable answer first and lets it be overridden second.

**Attribution stays visible.** Where artwork carries a watermark — the 97 Creative Management mark on the African people's work, for instance — the crop and the gradient must leave it intact.

**The gradient must:** start subtly above the text block; deepen toward the bottom; leave the upper image fully clear; adapt to the artwork rather than applying a flat heavy overlay; keep readable contrast at every size.

### The popup — expanded listing

Clicking the outer-edge action opens a popup over the floor the visitor is standing on: the larger artwork, shown whole rather than cropped; the full description; what the project or organisation actually does; an *Open Website* / *Explore* link to the real destination; optional contact or participation information.

The popup is not a departure. It does not move the lift, does not change the floor, and does not take the visitor out of the building until they choose the destination link themselves. Closing it puts them back on exactly the floor and wing they came from — same lift, same position, same two wings. A visitor who opens a project and changes their mind has lost nothing.

**What the popup carries, at minimum.** The larger image shown whole; the full description, long enough to explain what the project actually offers; the project's logo or other identity mark; the link to the project's own website; and any other destination already recorded for that record. The full description is never cut down to fit the frame — the frame says what the space is, the popup explains it.

**Explore is active only when its popup exists.** The action belongs to the frame from the first day the frame is drawn, but it is drawn **inert** until `js/project-display.js` can open and close the popup over the current floor. Nothing in the building ever looks pressable and does nothing: a control that cannot yet act is disabled and says so by its state, and it is never quietly deleted from the frame to avoid the question (decision 66).

### The project entry — one shape for every project

Every entry, in either wing, supports the same fields: a **project id**, the **wing-entry id** it is placed under, an **image reference**, a **title**, a **short description**, a **full description**, a **destination URL**, an optional **text / accent colour**, and a **display order**. The wing-entry id is what makes two appearances of the same project independent; the display order is what lets a wing be rearranged without touching the building.

The image is uploaded only after the frame structure has been established. A frame with no image is still a frame — with its gradient, its text positions and its outer-edge action — which is exactly what the empty building must prove before a single artwork arrives.

### The landmark frame

On the final destination floor the same component is used at full wing width: artwork shown whole, a longer description, one action. It is the same frame, larger — not a second design.

### An existing website is a display, not a floor
97 Creative Management already exists as one website presenting its own 97 Arts Galaxy showcase, so nothing is rebuilt, split or given a level of its own. It arrives as one project entry: a PNG, a description, and its existing link. Contact: `9797@gmail.com`.

That rule keeps the building from filling up with rooms that are really just links, and it prevents the same project appearing twice under two names.

**The wing heading names the space; the frame names the project.** On the first intermediate floor the right wing is headed 97 Arts Galaxy because that is the showcase on display, and the frame inside it is 97 Creative Management — one website, one entry, one link. Two labels, one destination.

### The template is universal

The same component serves community projects, businesses, podcasts, entertainment, ticketed experiences and creative showcases. One frame, one popup, many occupants.

## 11. Marquee and announcements

The marquee is a communication surface with its own dedicated track at the base of the building — not decoration, and not a second navigation menu.

| Kind | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Message** | Plain scrolling text. No link, no cursor change, nothing clickable. |
| **Link** | Scrolls as text but is genuinely clickable. Visually distinguishable as a link. |

**One voice each.** The marquee announces what is happening in the community, the circular control says where the visitor can go, and the floor indicator says where the visitor is. The marquee is never a navigation menu, the control never carries announcements, and the indicator never lists projects (decision 50). When it is unclear what an element is for, this is the rule that answers it.

**The circle belongs to the strip.** The lift control's circle sits at the left end of the marquee band, and the strip begins at it and flows to the right, sharing its gold hairline and its illuminated separators so the two read as one object (decision 56). The band stays inside the lift's width.

**The marquee is not the floor indicator, and not the navigation.** It announces; it never says which floor the visitor is on and never opens the lift's destination list. Announcements as a *destination* belong to the control, which opens the announcement panel without the lift moving (decision 52).

What it can carry: new projects entering the building, upcoming online events, free sessions, networking opportunities, community announcements, short positive expressions, links to external events.

**It must visibly carry the Gift of Remembrance** before a visitor reaches the final destination.

```
LINK     Free Gift of Remembrance
MESSAGE  Awareness creates room for understanding.
LINK     Upcoming Community Gathering
```

**Data shape:** the records described in §15, read only by `marquee.js`. `href` present means a link; absent means plain text. That is the entire rule, and it is the reason the marquee never turns into a second navigation menu.

## 12. Participation, membership and partnership

Three things that must stay distinct.

| Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Community participation** | Connect, take part in activities, share ideas, help bring awareness — no building space required |
| **Membership and active contribution** | Participate actively, contribute to activities, bring people in, support the network's growth |
| **Partnership and building presence** | Collaborate, contribute a project, or occupy a promotional / project space |
| **Financial arrangement (where applicable)** | Transparent, mutually agreed, value-creating for both sides — never an arbitrary charge |

**Ways to support that are not money:** sharing the link, introducing others, promoting projects, contributing skills and ideas, participating in events, collaborating on initiatives. Financial support is one option among these, not the entry ticket.

**The founding principles:** no racism; no prejudice or discrimination; no hate; no exploitation or forced participation; no performance or pretence in place of authenticity; openness to genuine collaboration; respect for free will and individual expression; partnerships where both sides have room to benefit and grow.

Being present in the building is not membership. Visitors are free to explore and free to leave.

> **The 150 / 2,000 figures were removed, not postponed.** They appeared in an earlier conversational transcription and were never established as amounts, prices, fees or membership terms. They are not confirmed project requirements and must not be used in the building, the membership structure, pricing or documentation. No decision is pending on them (decision 62), so nothing here is waiting to be answered — and no price is published anywhere in the building.

### The membership form — five questions, no more

1. Who you are.
2. What you want to contribute.
3. How you would like to participate.
4. Interest in partnership / collaboration / membership / another form of support.
5. Any necessary agreement or payment arrangement.

A conversation, not an application.

### Register / Join the Network — the collaboration form

Register / Join is its own destination, separate from membership. It is for someone who wants a project space, not merely to take part: a left-wing or right-wing space, an existing website or body of work, a service or project announcement, resources, knowledge, time, expertise, an idea, an opportunity, or a form of contribution nobody has listed yet. Contribution is not restricted to money (decision 54).

**The welcome, in the user's words:**

> Your free will matters here.
>
> You are not joining to fit into someone else's hierarchy. You are expressing your willingness to contribute, collaborate and grow in a space founded on love, respect and mutual benefit.
>
> There is no compulsory contribution format.
>
> Tell us how you would like to participate, what you would like to contribute, and what kind of growth you hope to create together.

The form is intentionally short. Five fields, all required: **name**, **email address**, **how would you like to participate**, **what would you like to contribute**, **what would make this collaboration mutually beneficial**. The participation field may offer suggestions but must still accept free text; the contribution field stays open-ended.

Before submission there is a plain acknowledgement: participation is voluntary, and the collaboration must respect mutual benefit, dignity, peace and freedom from prejudice. The form must never imply that money has bought ownership, or that any contribution guarantees a permanent wing. Proposals are reviewed and agreed by both parties before a space is established.

**The governing agreement.** Joining is an agreement to pursue mutual growth, and both sides must be able to name the value they receive. Contributions are never built on exploitation, compulsory payment or an unequal arrangement presented as partnership. The principles behind it: respect for human dignity; standing against racism and prejudice; working toward peace and love; respecting individual freedom and free will; supporting genuine collaboration; and recognising that different contributions create different forms of value.

## 14. Visual language and atmosphere

### The building is not themed after its destinations

One destination may carry a cosmic name; that does not make the architecture cosmic. The building should feel *elegant, calm and welcoming* — a real place with good light.

### Palette — settled

| Role | Colour | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **Dominant wall** | Warm ivory | Wing walls, elevator interior, surrounding stage |
| **Structure** | Champagne silver | Elevator framing, seams, frame edges |
| **Detail** | Brushed champagne gold | Floor indicator, door edges, fine rules — small quantities only |
| **Surface** | Clear glass | The elevator capsule and its reflections |
| **Supporting neutral** | Soft ash | Recesses, shadow, edges — never a large wall area |
| **Visitor accent** | The visitor's chosen colour | Their name, welcome portrait, a few personal accents — nowhere else |

Warm ivory dominates. The building keeps this palette permanently; the visitor's colour never spreads beyond their own elements.

*Starting values, to be tuned against your PNGs rather than treated as final: ivory `#F6F1E7`, champagne silver `#CFCBC2`, brushed gold `#C8A96A`. The artwork decides the final values.*

### Decoration — settled

Delicate petals drifting occasionally · soft architectural lighting · gentle reflections on the glass · beautiful framed artwork. Atmosphere, not spectacle.

**Deliberately excluded:** continuous ambient motion, particles filling the screen, glow behind text, anything that moves while the visitor is reading.

### The words

The typewriter text reaches for: **awareness, connection, understanding, possibility, expression, love.** The language of the building, not marketing copy.

The pages also state the negative half of the rule: no project name, description, link or image path is written into the HTML, because those belong to the records and a redraw would lose them.

### Where the building lives

The application is one repository: HTML for structure, `css/` for appearance, `js/` for data and behaviour, `data/` for records, `assets/` for pictures. GitHub Pages serves that repository as the building, so the repository is the source of truth — there is no second copy of the content living inside a builder somewhere else (decision 63).

Images live in `assets/projects/`, `assets/logos/`, `assets/entrance/` and `assets/elevator/`, and a record names its picture by path — `assets/projects/community-hub.png`. Nothing is embedded as Base64 and no record carries picture data, so replacing an image is replacing a file (decision 64). A record keeps two paths because the two display states show the same work at different sizes: `frameArtwork` for the frame and `popupArtwork` for the popup (§10). Either can stand while the other is still missing, and the frame keeps its shape either way.

The repository's own public address — the account and repository name that GitHub Pages serves — is not written into this document because it has not been supplied. Nothing here guesses it, and nothing depends on it: every path is repository-relative, so it resolves wherever the repository is served.

### Where the geometry lives

The proportions in §6 are custom properties at the top of `architecture.css`: spine width, wing widths, capsule width and height, grid columns and rows, gutter, padding, marquee band — set to 36% / 32% / 32%, 78% × 74%, 3 × 2 and 48px. `floor-renderer.js` reads the grid from those variables rather than keeping its own copy, so there is one source of truth — the one the browser is actually using.

### Engineering principles

- The spine is a component; the wings are components; neither imports the other's layout.
- A new floor is data. A new project is data plus artwork. Neither is a rebuild.
- The geometry lives in `architecture.css`; no component invents its own proportion.
- Floor positions and indicator numbers are derived from the floor array, so adding a floor needs no elevator rewrite.
- The lift's proportions and animation are independent of how many floors exist.
- Removing or replacing a project leaves every other slot untouched.
- A wing's entries are its own records; the project library is shared and read-only from the wing's side.
- A project's action sits at the outer edge of its frame; nothing interactive faces the lift except the lift's own control.
- A project's floor and side exist only where they are written down. Nothing is shown in the lift control because someone mentioned it.
- Load the architecture first; load project artwork as needed. WebP, lazy loading, CSS transitions rather than animation libraries.
- The visitor's colour is session preference — no account needed to enter.
- Modular CSS. External JS. No framework, no 3D engine until the need is demonstrated.

## 16. Responsive behaviour

**Desktop** — the whole cross-section: both wings visible at once, the lift travelling vertically in the centre, circular navigation floating in the spine, marquee on the base track.

**Mobile** — the spine becomes the full width of the screen and never becomes a menu. The wings wait off-stage left and right. The floor indicator stays visible at all times, and the circular control stays at the left of the marquee band in the lift view, never covering a wing indicator. The band itself never grows beyond the lift's width, even though the lift now fills the screen, and it never stretches across a wing when one slides in. It should still feel like standing inside the same building, only with less of it in view.

Three views, one building:

| View | What the visitor sees |
|---|---|
| **Lift** | The default. The capsule fills the screen, with the current floor, the indicator and the two destinations beside it |
| **Left wing** | Slides in from the left and replaces the lift in the viewing area; a persistent control returns to the lift |
| **Right wing** | The same, from the right |

**The wings announce themselves.** Two indicators sit on opposite sides of the lift presentation, one per wing, each naming its wing and previewing what is inside it. Selecting one slides that wing into view. A horizontal swipe does the same, so the visitor never needs instructions. A persistent return-to-lift control is always available, and the indicators update with the floor — when the lift rises, both previews change to what is actually on that floor. The visitor should never wonder whether they are looking at a different floor or a different room.

**Two directions, never confused.** Moving to a wing is horizontal; moving to a floor is vertical and upward in the lift. A wing never scrolls vertically like another floor, and the lift never slides sideways like a wing. The two movements are what tell the visitor which kind of place they have entered.

**The action stays at the edge when a wing arrives.** A wing reaches the visitor by sliding in horizontally. Its project's action button stays at the outer edge of the frame — far left for the left wing, far right for the right wing — and does not migrate toward the centre as the wing settles into view. Choosing it opens the popup over that wing, and closing it returns the visitor to the same wing and floor (decision 55).

This is a different arrangement, not the desktop automatically compressed (decision 42). No three-column compression, no shrinking the lift to a strip, no hiding the indicator to make room.

**Tablet** — spine 36% is preserved, wings narrow to 28% each with a 2 × 2 grid (four slots). The building keeps its proportions; only the gallery shrinks.

This is a building seen through narrower glass, not a different building.

### Duplication — the one decision, now made

The capability described is that **each terminal can duplicate a wing and its contents independently.** That is a structural capability, and it had two possible meanings which lead to different data:

- **Duplication by reference.** The new wing points at the same project entries as its source. Editing a project changes it on every floor that shows it. The duplicate is literally the same room seen from another level.
- **Duplication by copy.** The new wing receives its own entries, seeded from the source and then free to diverge. Editing one leaves the other untouched.

This mattered because a floor record storing a wing's contents as a bare list of project ids — `projects: ['…']` in earlier versions of §15 — *was* the reference model. Under copy-on-duplicate a wing needs its own entries and a stored origin id, and the record shape has to be decided before `floors.js` is written, not patched afterwards.

**It is now decided: duplication by copy.** A wing is a *space*, and spaces are renovatable independently (decisions 11 and 12); reference-duplication would produce two rooms that can never differ. The record shape that follows from it is set out in §7 and decision 40; the operation is `duplicateWing()` in `js/floors.js`.

The two meanings are kept here rather than deleted, because they are the reason the entry level exists at all.

## 22. Design principles to preserve

1. **Spatial logic first** — the building's structure is decided before its decoration.
2. **Central structure, branching wings** — the spine is the experience; the wings are what it opens onto.
3. **Architectural elegance** — the building is the identity, not a collection of adverts.
4. **Visual breathing space** — frames, controls and marquee each have their own room.
5. **Progressive discovery** — information appears when reached or requested, never before.
6. **Personal expression** — the visitor's colour belongs to them alone.
7. **Expandable architecture** — new projects and floors without structural change.
8. **Fixed identity, renovatable content** — the building stays; the spaces change.
9. **The image is the hero** — gradient carries information, popup carries detail.
10. **Lightweight development** — external JS, modular CSS, reusable templates, data files.
11. **Genuine collaboration** — mutually beneficial, freely chosen, honestly agreed.
12. **Nobody arrives at a wall** — the visitor is always free to explore, decline or leave.
13. **One voice per element** — the marquee announces, the logo navigates, the indicator locates. An element that does two jobs competes with itself.
14. **The interaction stays at the outer edge** — a wing's action faces away from the lift, so the centre is never crowded and the two wings never reach toward each other.
15. **Real names, recorded explicitly** — no project is invented, substituted or given a floor by guess. An assignment exists when it is written down, and not before.
16. **Assets are files, not data** — a picture is referenced by its path, so it can be replaced, renamed or re-designed without touching what any record means.
17. **Nothing pretends to work** — a control becomes active when the thing behind it exists, and stays visibly inert until then.
18. **The page names the file** — every HTML page says, where the change is made, which record or stylesheet owns it. An edit point is labelled where it stands, never discovered by reading the code.
